The Problem

A Gap in Scrutiny

Why Greenwashing is Now a Boardroom Risk.

Regulators are increasingly categorising environmental claims as greenwashing where they are vague, exaggerated, or unsubstantiated — closing the gap between corporate ambition and verifiable action.

Regulatory Focus

Regulators such as the CMA and ASA, applying the CAP Code, are shifting from voluntary guidance to strictly enforced disclosure. They now scrutinise:

  • The clarity of claims
  • The context in which they are presented
  • How they are understood by consumers
  • The evidence supporting them

The Compliance Standard

It is no longer enough for a claim to be technically true in a narrow sense. Environmental communication must also be:

  • Clear and unambiguous
  • Avoid misleading by omission
  • Reflect the appropriate scope of the product or service
  • Based on verifiable, contemporaneous evidence

Your Exposure

01

The Substantiation Trap

Broad, aspirational statements such as "Net Zero" or "Sustainable" made without an internal evidence repository that survives an audit are being flagged by regulators as inherently deceptive. Every claim must be supported by data accessible at the point of sale.

02

Misleading by Omission

Highlighting a single 'green' feature while ignoring significant environmental impacts elsewhere in the supply chain is no longer viable. Total lifecycle transparency is becoming the new legal baseline.

03

Compliance Lag

Marketing cycles move quicker than legal and ESG teams can verify data, leading to unqualified claims that can attract ASA/CAP sanctions. Alignment between creative and compliance teams is now a strategic necessity.

The Framework

CMA's Six-Point Test.

Precision over Persuasion

We align your commercial output with the CMA Green Claims Code. Our consultancy ensures that every statement satisfies the six fundamental principles of the new regulatory framework.

  1. 01

    Truthful and Accurate

    Move beyond halo effects and into factual precision.

  2. 02

    Clear and Unambiguous

    Ensure consumers aren't misled by technical jargon.

  3. 03

    No Omission of Material Information

    Provide the full picture of environmental impacts.

  4. 04

    Fair and Meaningful Comparisons

    Defend your "greenest" or "more sustainable" assertions.

  5. 05

    Contextually Complete

    Prove that a benefit in one section of the product's lifecycle is not offset by negative impacts in another.

  6. 06

    Full Substantiation

    Build the contemporaneous evidence packs needed to mount a defence.

Evidence must exist at the point a claim is made and not be developed retroactively.

The 'Red Zone' Terminology

Is your vocabulary creating liability?

In the current regulatory environment, "absolute" claims must be accompanied by absolute proof. We specialise in de-risking high-stakes terminology.

  • 01Net Zero
  • 02Carbon Neutral
  • 03Sustainable
  • 04Eco-Friendly
  • 05Responsibly Sourced

Such terms often call for:

  • Refining the scope and boundaries
  • Transparent methodology
  • Evidentiary support
  • Clearly explaining limitations

Without this, they may be considered misleading due to ambiguity or lack of substantiation.
