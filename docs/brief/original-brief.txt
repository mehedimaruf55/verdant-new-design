THE LEAF: the core idea
The logo mark is a two-leaf sprout: a smaller leaf pointing left and a larger leaf pointing up-right, joined by a short curved stem that tails down to the right. Each leaf has a thin slit-shaped cutout through its centre (a negative-space vein). In the logo it is filled with a left-to-right gradient, #316535 → #67B545.
Use the attached SVG exactly. Do not redraw it or swap in a generic leaf icon. Build the whole visual identity from this one mark, always as crisp vector:
- Hero centrepiece: the sprout very large and cropped off the right edge. On load, its outline draws in as a thin stroke, then fills, then the two slits open. Slow and elegant.
- Single-leaf module: split the sprout into its two leaves and use one leaf as a reusable shape
  - tiny single leaves as bullets/list markers
  - the 01–06 index numbers set inside small single-leaf shapes
  - card and image corners: one corner pointed like the leaf tip, the opposite corner rounded, the other two square
- The slit cutout: reuse that thin pointed slit as a graphic stroke, e.g. underline/strike-through on the Red Zone terms and as accent dashes
- The stem curve: use the stem's S-curve as a thin section-divider line ending in a small leaf
- Nav scroll indicator: a small sprout beside the logo that fills from bottom to top as you scroll
- Button/link hover: the arrow grows a small leaf
- Footer: an oversized sprout outline (stroke only, no fill) cropped off the bottom edge
Do NOT: scatter faded rotated leaf watermarks behind sections, use leaf photos, or use green gradient blobs or glowing orbs. The old site did that, and it's exactly what we're moving away from.

2. THE PROBLEM
Eyebrow: The Problem. H2: A Gap in Scrutiny
H3: Why Greenwashing is Now a Boardroom Risk.
Body: Regulators are increasingly categorising environmental claims as greenwashing where they are vague, exaggerated, or unsubstantiated — closing the gap between corporate ambition and verifiable action.
Stats: 3 — Core Exposure Risks · 6 — CMA Principles · 100% — Substantiation Required
Regulatory Focus: Regulators such as the CMA and ASA, applying the CAP Code, are shifting from voluntary guidance to strictly enforced disclosure. They now scrutinise: the clarity of claims; the context in which they are presented; how they are understood by consumers; the evidence supporting them.
The Compliance Standard: It is no longer enough for a claim to be technically true in a narrow sense. Environmental communication must also be: clear and unambiguous; avoid misleading by omission; reflect the appropriate scope of the product or service; based on verifiable, contemporaneous evidence.
Your Exposure (3 cards):
- The Substantiation Trap: Broad, aspirational statements such as "Net Zero" or "Sustainable" made without an internal evidence repository that survives an audit are being flagged by regulators as inherently deceptive. Every claim must be supported by data accessible at the point of sale.
- Misleading by Omission: Highlighting a single 'green' feature while ignoring significant environmental impacts elsewhere in the supply chain is no longer viable. Total lifecycle transparency is becoming the new legal baseline.
- Compliance Lag: Marketing cycles move quicker than legal and ESG teams can verify data, leading to unqualified claims that can attract ASA/CAP sanctions. Alignment between creative and compliance teams is now a strategic necessity.
Pull quote: Greenwashing is a Governance Failure, Not a Marketing Choice.

3. THE FRAMEWORK
Eyebrow: The Framework. H2: CMA's Six-Point Test. Sub: Precision over Persuasion
Body: We align your commercial output with the CMA Green Claims Code. Our consultancy ensures that every statement satisfies the six fundamental principles of the new regulatory framework.
01 Truthful and Accurate — Move beyond halo effects and into factual precision.
02 Clear and Unambiguous — Ensure consumers aren't misled by technical jargon.
03 No Omission of Material Information — Provide the full picture of environmental impacts.
04 Fair and Meaningful Comparisons — Defend your "greenest" or "more sustainable" assertions.
05 Contextually Complete — Prove that a benefit in one section of the product's lifecycle is not offset by negative impacts in another.
06 Full Substantiation — Build the contemporaneous evidence packs needed to mount a defence.
Callout: Evidence must exist at the point a claim is made and not be developed retroactively.

4. THE RED ZONE (the one deep-colour block)

Such terms often call for: refining the scope and boundaries; transparent methodology; evidentiary support; clearly explaining limitations.
Note: Without this, they may be considered misleading due to ambiguity or lack of substantiation.
